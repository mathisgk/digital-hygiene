Briefing 12 | Threat class: network attacks

Home network and public Wi-Fi

Mission objective. Get a realistic, not fear-based, model of network risk.

Field report

  • Router basics that matter: change the default admin password, update firmware (replace routers the maker stopped updating), turn off remote management and WPS, use WPA3 or WPA2-AES with a long passphrase.
  • Put guests and smart-home devices on a guest network, away from laptops and phones.
  • Every smart device is a computer you cannot fully control. Buy fewer. Do not fill the house with them, and think hard before putting your DNA in a corporate database.
  • Do not port-forward cameras to the internet. Use the vendor's cloud with MFA, or a VPN into your home network.
  • Public Wi-Fi: HTTPS already protects most page content, so sniffing is less of a threat than old advice implied. The real risks are fake “evil twin” networks and fake captive-portal login pages. Confirm the network name with staff, use your phone hotspot when unsure, and treat a VPN as an added layer.

Myth versus reality

Myth“Hiding my SSID and filtering MAC addresses secures my Wi-Fi.”
RealityNeither does anything real.
