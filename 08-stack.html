Briefing 08 | Threat class: tool sprawl

The privacy stack: pick one and stop

Mission objective. Leave with one coherent setup, not a pile of tools.

Field report

  • Rank by actual impact: (1) browser choice, (2) a content blocker, (3) an always-on VPN. A VPN alone is not a privacy strategy.
  • Windows: Brave (LibreWolf if you need Firefox), Brave Shields, Proton VPN always on with NetShield, Mullvad Browser for sensitive sessions. Mac and iPhone: Safari, Wipr 2, Proton VPN always on with NetShield, Mullvad Browser or Tor Browser for sensitive sessions.
  • Why the Mac stack is legitimate: Apple earns its money from hardware, not ad data. Safari integrates with every Apple device you own, so you use it daily without friction. Consistency is the most underrated privacy tool.
  • Its honest limits: Safari's content-blocking rules are more restricted than uBlock Origin on Firefox. CNAME cloaking (a tracker disguised as a first-party request) slips past Wipr, and NetShield is what catches it, so the VPN has to stay on. Apple's OS 26 URL-filtering changes are narrowing the gap.
  • iCloud Private Relay does not stack with a VPN. Apple pauses it while a VPN is active. It is also an iCloud+ feature that covers Safari and Mail, not all traffic. With an always-on VPN, Private Relay is moot. Choose one.
  • Minimalist rule: one daily browser, one sensitive-session browser, one VPN, one DNS layer. If your setup needs a maintenance schedule, it is too much. That is why Arkenfox is a skip.

Myth versus reality

Myth“More tools means more privacy.”
RealityPast a point, more tools means more breakage and more chores, and you quit.
