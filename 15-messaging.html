Briefing 15 | Threat class: message interception

Messaging apps and encryption

Mission objective. Know what “encrypted” covers, pick apps on purpose, and lock them down.

Field report

  • End-to-end encryption (E2EE) means only sender and recipient hold the keys. The server operator cannot read it.
  • Data has three states, and each gets a different protection. In transit (moving between your phone and the server): protected by TLS, the same encryption behind HTTPS. It stops anyone on the Wi-Fi or along the network path. The company's server still sees the plaintext. At rest (sitting on a disk): protected by device encryption on your phone and server-side encryption in the company's data center. It stops a thief with a stolen phone or drive. If the company holds the keys, the company can still read the data and can be compelled to hand it over. End to end: only the two phones hold the keys, so the message is unreadable in transit and unreadable on the server. Ask who holds the keys.
  • Picture it: transit encryption is an armored truck, at-rest encryption is a vault the company holds the key to, and E2EE is a sealed envelope only the recipient can open.
  • E2EE ends at the endpoints. Once a message lands, its protection at rest on your phone is only as good as your passcode and device encryption. Cloud backups are the usual hole: iCloud without Advanced Data Protection, and WhatsApp backups unless you turn on encrypted backups.
  • Content and metadata are different. Metadata is who, when, how often, from where. An app can encrypt content and still hand the owner a map of your social life.
  • Signal: E2EE by default for messages, calls, and groups, minimal metadata, nonprofit, open source. The recommendation for sensitive talk. Use usernames so people do not see your number. Turn on disappearing messages and set a registration PIN.
  • WhatsApp: same protocol as Signal, on by default. Meta owns it and sees metadata. Instructor position: not for sensitive communication.
  • iMessage: E2EE between Apple devices. The weak spot is iCloud backup. Turn on Advanced Data Protection.
  • iPhone to Android (RCS): E2EE began rolling out in May 2026 with iOS 26.5 and the latest Google Messages. It is labeled beta, depends on the carrier, and shows a lock icon when active. Look for the lock instead of assuming.
  • Telegram: not E2EE by default. Regular chats and all group chats are readable by Telegram's servers. Only one-on-one Secret Chats are end to end. Plain SMS: no encryption, spoofable, tied to SIM swapping.
  • Hygiene: lock-screen previews off, review linked devices (attackers trick people into scanning a QR code that links their device to your account), never share a verification code, and treat unknown numbers and surprise group invites as the start of a scam. Minimalist rule: two messaging apps, chosen on purpose.

Myth versus reality

Myth“It is encrypted, so it is safe.”
RealityEncrypted means the pipe is private. It says nothing about who is on the other end.
