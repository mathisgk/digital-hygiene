Glossary

3-2-1 rule briefing 18

Three copies of your data, on two kinds of media, with one copy offsite.

Account recovery briefing 04

The reset path (email, phone, backup codes) that lets you back into an account. Attackers abuse it.

AI chat history briefing 16

Saved conversations with an AI tool. Treat them like email: they can be breached or accessed through your account.

Always-on VPN briefing 06

Leaving the VPN connected all the time so protection is a habit and not a decision.

Attack surface briefing 01

Everything an attacker could try to reach: your accounts, apps, extensions, devices, and habits. Smaller is safer.

Auto-update briefing 09

A setting that installs patches for you so safety does not depend on remembering.

Clean device briefing 19

A device you trust is uncompromised, used to change passwords and review accounts.

ClickFix briefing 14

A fake CAPTCHA or error page that tricks you into pasting and running a malicious command yourself.

CNAME cloaking briefing 08

A tracker hiding behind a first-party subdomain so ordinary blockers miss it. DNS-level blocking can catch it.

Containment briefing 19

Stopping the damage from spreading, such as disconnecting a device from the network.

Credential stuffing briefing 02

Automatically trying passwords leaked from one site on many other sites.

Credit freeze briefing 17

A free lock on your credit file that stops new accounts from being opened in your name until you lift it.

Data breach briefing 02

When a company's stored user data is stolen. Your risk depends on whether the stolen password was unique.

Data broker briefing 16

A company that collects and sells personal data such as your name, address, and relatives.

Deepfake briefing 14

AI-generated audio or video that imitates a real person.

Defense in depth briefing 01

Layering prevention, detection, and recovery so that no single failure leaves you exposed.

DNS briefing 07

Domain Name System. Translates names like example.com into the IP addresses computers use.

DNS filtering briefing 07

Blocking known tracker and malware domains at lookup time, before your browser ever connects.

DNS leak briefing 07

DNS lookups that escape the VPN tunnel and reach your ISP, exposing the sites you visit.

DoH and DoT briefing 07

DNS over HTTPS and DNS over TLS. Two ways to encrypt DNS lookups so the network between you and the resolver cannot read them.

Email alias briefing 04

A forwarding address you give to one site so your real address stays private.

Encryption at rest briefing 15

Protection of stored data on a disk or server. If the company holds the keys, the company can still read it.

Encryption in transit briefing 15

Protection while data moves across the network, usually TLS. The server at the end still sees plaintext.

End of life (EOL) briefing 09

The date a vendor stops providing security updates. After it, every new flaw stays open.

End-to-end encryption briefing 15

Only the sender and recipient hold the keys, so the service in the middle cannot read the message.

Entry point briefing 19

The way the attacker got in. Find and fix it, not just the symptom.

Evil twin briefing 12

A fake Wi-Fi network with a trusted-looking name, set up to capture logins or traffic.

Extended Security Updates (ESU) briefing 09

Paid or limited security-only updates for an out-of-support product. A bridge, not a destination.

Facial recognition briefing 16

Software that identifies people from photos. Rally and protest photos can expose participants.

Family safe word briefing 14

A pre-agreed word a caller must know before any money moves, used to defeat voice-clone scams.

Fingerprinting briefing 05

Identifying you by the combination of your device, fonts, screen, and settings, without any cookie.

Full-disk encryption briefing 10

Scrambling everything on a drive so a stolen laptop is unreadable without the login. BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on Mac.

Geotagging briefing 16

Location data embedded in photos and posts that can reveal where you live or travel.

Guest network briefing 12

A separate Wi-Fi network for visitors and smart devices, isolated from your computers and phones.

Have I Been Pwned briefing 04

A free service that tells you whether your email appears in known data breaches and can alert you to new ones.

Host key briefing 20

The key (Right Ctrl by default in VirtualBox) that releases your mouse and keyboard from a VM and triggers VM shortcuts.

HTTPS-Only mode briefing 05

A browser setting that refuses unencrypted connections and warns you before loading a plain HTTP site.

iCloud Private Relay briefing 08

An iCloud+ privacy feature that splits who you are from where you go. It pauses when a VPN is on.

Irreversible payment briefing 17

Gift cards, crypto, and wire transfers. Money sent this way is almost impossible to recover.

IRS IP PIN briefing 17

An Identity Protection PIN that blocks anyone else from filing a tax return with your Social Security number.

Kernel briefing 10

The core of an operating system that talks to hardware. Linux is a kernel.

Kill switch briefing 06

A VPN feature that blocks traffic if the tunnel drops, so nothing leaks outside it.

Least privilege briefing 01

Giving every user, app, and extension only the access it needs. Daily work from a standard account is the everyday example.

Lockdown Mode briefing 11

An extreme iPhone setting for high-risk users that disables features to shrink the attack surface.

Maintenance burden briefing 08

The ongoing work a setup needs to stay working. Hardened configs with update scripts have a high one.

Malvertising briefing 05

Malware delivered through online ads. A big reason ad blockers count as security tools.

Metadata briefing 15

Data about the message: who, when, how often, from where. It can reveal a lot even when content is encrypted.

MFA briefing 03

Multi-factor authentication: proving who you are with something beyond a password.

No-logs policy briefing 06

A VPN's claim that it does not keep records of your activity. Look for independent audits that back it up.

Open source briefing 10

Software whose code anyone can read, modify, and share.

Passkey briefing 03

A phishing-resistant login that replaces the password with a cryptographic key stored on your device.

Passphrase briefing 02

Several random words used as a password. Long, memorable, and strong when the words are truly random.

Password manager briefing 02

An app that creates, stores, and fills unique passwords behind one strong master passphrase.

Patch briefing 09

A software update that fixes a security hole or bug.

Persistence briefing 04

A hidden foothold an attacker leaves behind, such as a forwarding rule, so they keep access after you change a password.

Phishing briefing 13

Fraudulent messages that trick you into giving up credentials, money, or access.

Port-out protection briefing 11

A carrier lock that stops anyone from moving your number to another carrier without your PIN.

Pretexting briefing 14

Inventing a believable scenario or identity to get someone to hand over access or information.

Quishing briefing 13

Phishing through QR codes that lead to a fake site.

Ransomware briefing 18

Malware that encrypts your files and demands payment for the key. Good backups make it survivable.

Reimage briefing 19

Wiping a device and reinstalling the operating system when malware may have run on it.

Restore test briefing 18

Actually recovering a file from a backup to prove the backup works.

Sandboxing briefing 11

Isolating each app so a compromised one cannot read another app's data.

Sensitive-session browser briefing 08

A separate browser with no logins or history, used only for private browsing, such as Mullvad Browser.

SIM swapping briefing 03

Tricking a carrier into moving your phone number to the attacker's SIM so they receive your text codes.

Smishing briefing 13

Phishing by text message (SMS).

Snapshot briefing 20

A saved state of a VM you can instantly return to.

Standard user account briefing 10

An everyday account without admin rights. Malware running as you can do less damage.

Stolen Device Protection briefing 11

An iPhone feature that adds delays and requires biometrics for sensitive changes when the phone is away from familiar places.

Sync vs backup briefing 18

Sync mirrors changes, including deletions and damage. A backup keeps older, recoverable versions.

Threat model briefing 01

A short written answer to: what am I protecting, from whom, how likely is the attack, how bad is the damage, and how much effort will I spend preventing it.

TOTP briefing 03

Time-based one-time password. The six-digit code an authenticator app shows and rotates every 30 seconds.

Virtual card briefing 17

A disposable card number tied to your real account, useful for risky checkouts.

Virtual machine briefing 20

A software computer running inside your real one, isolated from it.

Vishing briefing 13

Phishing by phone call, increasingly with AI-cloned voices.

VM escape briefing 20

A rare bug that lets code break out of a VM into the host. A reason to keep the host patched.

VPN briefing 06

A service that encrypts your connection to a server, which then forwards your traffic. It hides your IP from sites and your traffic from your ISP.

WebKit briefing 05

Apple's browser engine. Every browser on iPhone must use it, so iPhone browser choice is mostly cosmetic.

WPA3 briefing 12

The current Wi-Fi security standard. WPA2 with AES is the minimum acceptable.

WPS briefing 12

A Wi-Fi setup shortcut with known weaknesses. Turn it off.
