Briefing 05 | Threat class: tracking and malvertising

Browsers

Mission objective. Understand that security and privacy are different questions, and pick a browser on purpose.

Field report

  • Your browser is your biggest leak: IP address, device characteristics, trackers, cookies. A better browser cuts passive tracking. It does nothing about what you volunteer by logging into Google or Facebook.
  • Two questions. Security: is the engine patched and sandboxed? Privacy: what does it leak, and who profits? Chrome's engine is solid. Chrome's problem is its business model and no fingerprint protection.
  • Picks. Mac and iPhone: Safari plus Wipr 2. Windows: Brave, from brave.com only. If you need a Firefox-based browser: LibreWolf, from librewolf.net only. Sensitive sessions: Mullvad Browser, or Tor Browser when anonymity matters. Brave's Tor window is weaker than Tor Browser.
  • Skip. Chrome and Edge (no fingerprint protection), Vivaldi (partly closed source, Google APIs), Firefox plus Arkenfox (same fingerprint result as LibreWolf, plus a maintenance chore).
  • On iPhone every browser runs on Apple's WebKit engine. Brave, Firefox, and Chrome there are Safari with a different skin. Choose Safari.
  • Ad blocking is a security control, because ads deliver malware. Use uBlock Origin on Firefox-based browsers, uBlock Origin Lite on Chromium, Shields in Brave, Wipr on Safari.
  • Settings that matter: auto-update on, HTTPS-Only mode on, tracking protection on, extensions cut to what you actually use, and browser password saving off in favor of your manager.
  • Download software only from the vendor's own site. Fake download pages are a top malware route.

Myth versus reality

Myth“Incognito makes me anonymous.”
RealityIt only clears local history. Your ISP, network admin, and every site you visit still see you.

Field exercise

Run coveryourtracks.eff.org and browserleaks.com in two browsers and compare “unique” with “randomized.” Then open an extension's permissions and read them aloud.
