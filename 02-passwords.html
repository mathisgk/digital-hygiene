Briefing 02 | Threat class: credential theft

Passwords and password managers

Mission objective. Kill password reuse.

Field report

  • Reuse is the root problem. One breached site plus one reused password means every account sharing it is compromised. Attackers replay leaked passwords on other sites automatically. That is called credential stuffing.
  • Use a password manager: Bitwarden (free tier covers the job), 1Password, or KeePassXC (local file, no cloud). Skip LastPass: it suffered a serious breach in 2022 that exposed customers' encrypted vault backups.
  • The master password is a passphrase: several random words you can pronounce and remember. Generate it with dice or the manager. Never reuse it anywhere.
  • Do not rotate passwords on a schedule. Change one after a breach, a theft, or a phishing slip.
  • The manager flags reused and breached passwords. Run that report on day one.
  • Passwords saved in the browser beat reuse and lose to a real manager.

Myth versus reality

Myth“A password with symbols and numbers is the strong one.”
RealityLength and uniqueness beat complexity. A manager removes the debate because you never type or reuse a password.
