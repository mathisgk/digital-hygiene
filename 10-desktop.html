Briefing 10 | Threat class: device compromise

Desktop operating systems and hardening

Mission objective. Know the baseline for any computer you own.

Field report

  • Baseline for every desktop: standard user account for daily work, full-disk encryption (BitLocker or Device Encryption on Windows, FileVault on Mac), strong login, auto-lock, firewall on.
  • Antivirus: Defender is enough on Windows. Optional on Mac. Skip paid suites. A second-opinion on-demand scanner is fine.
  • Office macros: Microsoft blocks macros in files downloaded from the internet by default. Leave that on, and never click “Enable content” on a document you did not expect.
  • Windows telemetry: O&O ShutUp10++ (oo-software.com only) is optional. Minimalist view: skip it unless you will maintain it.
  • Physical security counts. Lock the screen, watch for shoulder surfers, and think twice about laptop stickers that announce your affiliations.
  • Security profiles: Windows is the biggest target and has the most telemetry. macOS has strong sandboxing and less noise. Linux has no OS-level telemetry but needs manual hardening, especially for physical security.
  • Linux in one slide. Started in 1991 by Linus Torvalds as a free alternative to restrictive UNIX. Linux is the kernel, not a whole OS. Open source: anyone can view, modify, and distribute the code. Runs with a GUI or none. Dominates supercomputers, web servers, public cloud, and mobile (Android), and runs smart-home devices, cars, spacecraft, Kindles, and the Steam Deck. On the desktop it holds only about 4%. Who it is for: developers, privacy-minded users, people reviving old hardware, security and community folks.
  • Linux is not automatically safe. “No telemetry” is not “hardened,” and it still needs updates.

Myth versus reality

Myth“Macs and Linux cannot get malware.”
RealityThey get less commodity malware. Phishing and fake installers work on every OS.
