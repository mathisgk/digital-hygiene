Briefing 13 | Threat class: phishing

Phishing, smishing, vishing, and quishing

Mission objective. Dissect a suspicious message in 30 seconds.

Field report

  • Phishing is still the top way into accounts and networks, ahead of software exploits.
  • Red flags: urgency or fear, a sender domain that does not match the display name, a generic greeting, and an ask for credentials, payment, or gift cards.
  • The “bad grammar” tell is dead. AI-written lures read clean. Check the domain and the ask.
  • Know the difference between link text and the real destination. Hover before you click, or better, never click: go to the site or app directly.
  • Quishing (QR-code phishing): scanning a random QR code is the same risk as clicking a random link.
  • Vishing and smishing (calls and texts) keep climbing. AI voice cloning can fake a family member or a boss. Any unexpected request involving money gets verified on a second channel, by calling back on a known number.
  • Attachments: do not open files from people you do not know or were not expecting. Preview an unexpected file in the browser or a cloud viewer instead of downloading it.
  • Top search results can be paid ads that impersonate real sites. Use bookmarks for banking and shopping.

Myth versus reality

Myth“I would know if it was fake.”
RealityThe best ones are indistinguishable at a glance.
