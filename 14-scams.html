Briefing 14 | Threat class: social engineering

Social engineering, scams, ClickFix, and AI fakes

Mission objective. Understand that the exploit is trust, not code.

Field report

  • Pretexting: someone posing as IT, a bank, a boss, or a coworker so you act before you think.
  • Romance and investment scams run on isolation and secrecy. “Do not tell your bank or your family” is the red flag.
  • ClickFix (fake CAPTCHA). A page says “verify you are human,” copies a hidden command to your clipboard, and tells you to press Windows+R (or open Terminal), paste, and press Enter. You just installed the malware yourself. CrowdStrike's 2026 threat report counted a 563% jump in fake-CAPTCHA incidents in 2025, and the FTC issued a consumer alert in June 2026. No real CAPTCHA ever asks you to open a system window or paste a command.
  • Deepfake audio and video are cheap now. Set a family safe word that a caller must know before any money moves.
  • Fake tech support, fake delivery texts, fake “your account is locked” pages, and fake invoices all use the same engine: urgency and fear.
  • One rule covers the lot: urgency plus secrecy plus money or access means stop.

Myth versus reality

Myth“Only gullible people fall for scams.”
RealityTargeted, researched scams work on careful people. That is why they pay.
