Briefing 06 | Threat class: ISP and network visibility

VPNs

Mission objective. Say exactly what a VPN does and does not do.

Field report

  • True or false: “A VPN makes you private.” False. A VPN is not an invisibility cloak.
  • What it does: encrypts the tunnel between you and the VPN server, and masks your real IP address from websites. Your ISP stops seeing your traffic. The VPN company now can.
  • What it does not do: stop fingerprinting, cookies, trackers, or login-based identification. Logged into Google on a VPN, Google knows exactly who you are.
  • Instructor position: leave it on everywhere. It takes your ISP and the local network out of the picture and turns “should I connect?” into a habit. The cost is real: you trust the VPN company instead of your ISP, so provider choice matters more than the category.
  • Pick a provider with an independently audited no-logs claim, open-source apps, and a kill switch. Proton VPN and Mullvad fit. Proton's free tier has unlimited data but fewer servers, and its NetShield tracker and malware blocking is paid only.
  • Skip other free VPNs. If the product is free, your traffic is the product. The best VPN is the one you will actually leave on.

Myth versus reality

Myth“A VPN makes me anonymous.”
RealityIt moves who can see you. It does not hide who you are.
