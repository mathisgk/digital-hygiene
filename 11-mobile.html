Briefing 11 | Threat class: phone theft and SIM swapping

Mobile security

Mission objective. Lock down the device that holds your whole life.

Field report

  • Security profiles. iOS: high (strongest sandboxing, fast centralized patches, native hardware encryption). Pixel: moderate (best Android updates, heavy Google telemetry). Other Android: low (delayed patches, manufacturer bloat). GrapheneOS: high (hardened Android, Pixel only, no Google Play Services by default, verified boot). Instructor position: if you can, get an iPhone. On Android, buy a Pixel.
  • Lock the phone. A PIN of six or more digits at minimum, alphanumeric better. Avoid patterns, birthdays, and addresses. Watch for shoulder surfing. Biometrics are fine unless legal coercion is in your threat model, in which case rely on the passcode.
  • Official app stores only. Do not jailbreak, do not sideload. Audit permissions (location, mic, contacts, photos) and delete apps you do not use.
  • iPhone settings to verify: Stolen Device Protection (Settings, Face ID and Passcode). Apple began pushing it to everyone in iOS 26.4, so confirm it is on. Advanced Data Protection (Settings, your name, iCloud) end-to-end encrypts iCloud backups and most synced data. Safari advanced tracking and fingerprinting protection (Settings, Apps, Safari, Advanced). Lockdown Mode is for high-risk users only, because it breaks features on purpose. Find My on.
  • Android: Play Protect on, Play Store only, stay on a supported model. Modern Android phones encrypt storage by default. Verify instead of assuming.
  • Antivirus: none exists for iPhone, and it is not needed on a Pixel. On an old Android, replacing the phone beats adding an app.
  • Lock up your phone number (SIM swapping). Move 2FA off SMS. Call your carrier and add an account PIN or passcode that must be given before any account change, plus port-out protection. For accounts that demand a number, consider a VoIP number (Google Voice) instead of your real one, and put strong MFA on that account.

Myth versus reality

Myth“My phone is safer than my laptop, so I do not have to think about it.”
RealityIt is safer by design and more exposed by use: always on, always with you, and full of logged-in apps.
