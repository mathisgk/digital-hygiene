Briefing 16 | Threat class: oversharing

Data you give away: social media, brokers, and AI tools

Mission objective. Audit your own exposure once, in class.

Field report

  • Assume everything you post is public, permanently.
  • Do not overexpose: no photos of credit cards or boarding passes, no geotagged posts, no public Venmo feed, no running routes that start at your front door. Turn privacy settings up on your top three platforms and skip the rest.
  • Be careful who you tag and photograph. Facial recognition makes protest, rally, and meeting photos a real risk. Get consent from the people in the frame. Activists should consider pseudonyms.
  • Do not put sensitive data into big corporate databases without need: consumer DNA kits, free “quiz” apps, loyalty programs with full profiles.
  • Data brokers sell your name, address, and relatives. Opt out of the main ones. Chasing every broker is a time sink with falling returns.
  • AI tools are a data-exposure channel. Do not paste passwords, keys, student records, client data, or anything you could not post publicly. Check each tool's settings for training use and chat retention. Treat chatbot history like email: it can be breached, subpoenaed, or seen by whoever gets into the account.
  • Normalize good habits. Ask friends before you post their photo.

Myth versus reality

Myth“I have nothing to hide.”
RealityTracking feeds price discrimination, profiling, scams that know your details, and breach damage.
