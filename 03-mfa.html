Briefing 03 | Threat class: account takeover

MFA, passkeys, and security keys

Mission objective. Every important account gets a second factor better than a text message.

Field report

  • Ranking, best to worst. 1: passkeys and hardware security keys (YubiKey, Titan), which are phishing-resistant because there is nothing to type into a fake site. 2: an authenticator app (TOTP codes). 3: SMS text codes, the weakest because of SIM swapping.
  • Authenticator options: Ente Auth, Aegis (Android), 2FAS, Microsoft Authenticator. Google Authenticator works, but its cloud sync is not end-to-end encrypted. Skip Authy: the desktop apps are gone and there is no export.
  • Turn it on first for email, your Apple or Google account, banking, the password manager, social media, and your phone carrier account.
  • Plan for loss. Buy two security keys, or pair one key with an authenticator app, and store printed recovery codes somewhere safe and offline. Most lockouts are self-inflicted.
  • MFA fatigue: never approve a prompt you did not start. A surprise prompt means someone already has your password.

Myth versus reality

Myth“MFA makes me unhackable.”
RealityLive phishing sites can relay codes in real time. Only passkeys and security keys resist that.

Field exercise

Try itSet up a passkey on a Google or Microsoft account live. Then open the recovery-code page and talk about where you would store the codes.
